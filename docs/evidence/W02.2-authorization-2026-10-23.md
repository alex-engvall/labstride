## Local behavior and recovery

`tests/policy.test.mjs` exercises the actual `ConfigurationStore`, `ExecutionStore`, workers and SQLite migration, not a parallel permission mock. Its provider identities are synthetic and its verifier is explicitly enrolled trusted test code. Tests cover:

- Rejection of serialized/lookalike/foreign capabilities and caller-supplied audit identities for configuration/policy changes and configured controls/budget renewal.
- Durable admission denial with no request/run/workspace/operation/outbox/delivery, replay of the original decision after reopening, every actor-evidence correlation field, repository ID/name matching and exact target/action/version grants.
- Issue/PR-like facts cannot publish policy or acquire permissions. Configured low-level `scheduleStart` cannot bypass verified admission with an allowed audit actor and valid snapshot.
- CAS, conflicting change IDs and lost-acknowledgement replay return the original policy receipt after later revisions and reopening. Invalid schemas and unmapped repository grants fail before policy writes.
- Active runs keep their original policy after ordinary publication. Only an explicit audited `bind_run` changes their policy revision. Immutable run configuration and artifact constraints still apply.
- Installation and run restrictions survive reopening. An asynchronous validation barrier permits a revocation before effect; the independent real target SQLite database stays empty. A restriction committed by another coordinator connection blocks the original claim's boundary.
- Follow-up scheduling is rejected before a second executable outbox is stored. Revocation also blocks cancellation submission, while an independent result read can still reconcile the original operation to success. Its original handle and historical admission decision remain available.
- Strict hooks cannot grant completion/approval or change policy. Read-only session refresh still works under revocation. Emergency pause prevents configured projection writes while inspection remains available.
- Digest-checked immutable policy records reject normal updates and detect simulated valid-JSON corruption before effects. A real v10 schema upgrade preserves checksums and configured run identities without inventing authorization.

A child process is killed with SIGKILL after restriction/state/change receipt writes inside the policy transaction, before commit. A fresh coordinator sees the original revision and no partial control change; applying the request commits one revision. Another child is killed after commit before its response: a fresh coordinator returns the same revision/receipt without applying again. Both cases deny admission after the recovered restriction.

The existing configured-admission process-loss tests now enroll policy authority and actor verification. They still kill actual child processes inside admission, after admission commit, before the installed effect and after its separate SQLite effect commits. Original operation/configuration/policy identities survive; unknown effects reconcile without invoking the action again. Existing W01 socket-based execution/cancellation/session tests also remain in the full suite.
